# KJubilee · HM 308.70 — Sweepers

**The between-songs brand. Ten of them.** A sweeper runs every two or three songs, so this
slot fires four or five times an hour against the top-of-hour ID's one. It is the most
repeated audio on the station and the fastest to wear out.

| | |
|---|---|
| Station | **KJubilee** |
| Frequency | **HM 308.70** — the Heavenly Modulation band |
| Slogan (primary) | **Where the whole house sings** |
| Length | **3–7 seconds.** Longer than seven and it stops being a sweeper. |
| Rhythm | Every 2–3 songs. Brand touches the ear 4–6× an hour including the top-of-hour ID. |
| Sonic signature | Three-note motif — see `branded/sonic-signature/` |

## ⚠ THE FREQUENCY IS 308.70

Five of the twenty sweepers already in `branded/sweepers/` say **388.70** — `sweeper-01`,
`-05`, `-10`, `-15`, `-19`. They predate the 2026-08-25 renumber. Every script in this file
says **308.70**. Do not generate from the old files.

**Paste the SAY line, not the numeral.** Suno reads `308.70` as "three hundred eight point
seven zero". House read: **"H-M three-oh-eight seventy"**.

## The named failure mode — read this before writing any more

From `branded/README.txt`, section 5.3:

> **Too few sweepers is worse than too few songs.** Listeners forgive hearing a song twice
> in a day. They notice the same eight-word stinger by the third hour.

Twenty is the launch minimum and this file is ten of them. **Ten is not enough on its own.**
Treat these as the strongest ten — the ones that define the voice — and keep the existing
set alongside them once the frequency is corrected. Refresh quarterly (11.3).

## Suno or TTS? Decide this first — it will save you hours

**Not every sweeper should be made in Suno.** A sweeper comes in two kinds and only one of
them wants a music generator:

**DRY** — voice alone, no music. These get dropped live over the instrumental intro of the
next song, so any music in the file fights the record underneath it. **Do not make these in
Suno.** Suno always produces music; getting a genuinely dry vocal out of it is a fight you
lose. Send these down the path the tree already has: a plain `.txt` in `branded/sweepers/`
containing **only the words**, fed to text-to-speech verbatim. That is exactly what the
station README specifies, and it is the right tool.

**WET** — voice plus the motif, a self-contained little piece of audio that plays between
two songs. **These are the Suno ones.**

Each script below is marked **DRY** or **WET**. Six wet, four dry. The dry ones still get
a persona and a delivery note so the TTS voice and the Suno voice stay recognisably the
same station.

## How to build the WET ones in Suno — step by step

Researched against Suno v5 / v5.5 and against this workspace's own audit
(`BLUEPRINT_AND_SUNO_AUDIT_REPORT_2026-06-03.md`), which is the house authority and is
stricter than the public guides. The full rule set is in
[`../station-identifiers/HM308.70.md`](../station-identifiers/HM308.70.md); what follows is
everything that matters for sweepers specifically.

### 1. Use one Voice for all of them

Make the Voice once from your best take (**⋯ → Create Persona**, free) and attach it to
every generation. Name it **`KJubilee — Jubilee (Host)`** — the same Voice the station IDs
use, so the top of the hour and the middle of the hour are the same station. In v5.5 the
**Voices** button replaced *Personas* in Create; Style Personas still live inside that menu.
A Voice gives you *the same act, not the same take* — which is what you want across
twenty sweepers.

### 2. Custom Mode, then paste both fields verbatim

- **Lyrics field** — 5,000 char limit; every sweeper here is under 120. **Short lyrics are
  the single strongest lever on duration**, and a sweeper is the shortest thing the station
  makes.
- **`[Spoken Word]`** is the documented spoken-delivery tag. `[Announcer]` is not a real
  tag — it gets sung about as often as it gets spoken. Put the announcer character in the
  Style field instead.
- **Production cues go inside the bracket**, after an em dash:
  `[Spoken Word — dry, close-mic, forward]`. Loose parentheses get sung as lyrics; the
  audit scrubbed thousands of them.
- **Tag whitelist:** `[Intro] [Hook] [Spoken Word] [Outro] [End]`. Nothing else belongs in
  something this short.
- **End with `[End]`** — it is what stops trailing audio. A sweeper with a tail is unusable.

### 3. Style field — 800 char house cap, first 200 load-bearing

Suno allows 1,000; this house caps at 800 and front-loads the field because Suno weights
the opening most heavily. Format and length constraint first, texture last. All ten Style
blocks below are pre-counted.

**Rule E — no artist or band names, ever.** The audit scrubbed 764 artist tokens from one
persona's files alone. Describe the sound, never who made it.

### 4. Getting 3–6 seconds, which is shorter than Suno wants to go

Same levers as the station IDs but turned up. In order of power:

| Phrase | Effect |
|---|---|
| `no verse, no chorus` | The strongest constraint there is. |
| `single musical phrase` | One melodic unit, no development. |
| `sting`, `logo jingle`, `broadcast bumper` | Genre keywords for programme audio, not music. **`sting` is the one that gets you under 7 seconds.** |
| `instant impact, no intro build-up` | Kills the run-up. |
| `clean ending hit` | Decisive stop, obvious edit point. |

**Words that will quietly turn a 4-second sweeper into a 3-minute song:** *cinematic, epic,
dramatic, build-up, rising intro, emotional intro, full song, complete track, radio edit,
emotional vocal, melodic vocal.*

**Do not over-instruct.** This is the specific trap for sweepers: piling on adjectives to
get precision makes Suno reach for structure and the thing gets longer. Keep the Style
field lean. If it will not come in short, cut words rather than adding them.

**And accept that you will trim in an editor.** Nobody hits an exact duration in Suno.
Generate four, keep the closest, top and tail it.

### 5. Exclude Styles, not negative style text

"no drums" inside the Style field is unreliable. The **Exclude Styles** box under Advanced
Options is the real control. Each script gives you one.

### 6. Carry the motif — and put the name last

Section 12.8: the signature motif appears in **every** sweeper. Each script names its
variant. And on something this short the **station name should be the final sound**, because
the tail is what the ear keeps.

### 7. Where the audio goes

`.mp3` into `branded/sweepers/`, same basename as its script. Match levels across the whole
set — a sweeper that is 3 dB hotter than the others is the one listeners notice, and not
kindly.

## The ten

Ordered by how hard they work. **SW-01 to SW-04 carry the load** — name, frequency, slogan,
usable any hour. The rest add texture, daypart and personality so the set does not wear
through by hour three.

### SW-01 · "The Stamp" — **WET** — the shortest thing the station says

Name and frequency, nothing else, over the motif. This is the one that runs most often. It
has no personality on purpose: there is nothing here to get tired of.

**Persona:** Jubilee Inspire — spoken, warm, forward · **Target:** 0:03–0:04 · **Motif:** `signature-short`

**SAY:** H-M three-oh-eight seventy

```
[Intro — two-note piano motif, warm, immediate]

[Spoken Word — close-mic, warm mezzo-soprano, forward, no pause]
KJubilee. H-M three-oh-eight seventy.

[Outro — motif resolves, clean stop]
[End]
```

**Style of Music** *(267 chars)*
```
Radio sweeper sting, broadcast bumper, single musical phrase, no verse, no chorus, instant impact, no intro build-up, clean ending hit, very short. Warm female mezzo-soprano announcer, spoken not sung, close-mic, dry, forward. Two-note warm piano motif with soft pad.
```

**Exclude Styles**
```
drums, percussion, beat, singing, choir, applause, sound effects, fade out, long reverb
```

### SW-04 · "All Worship. No Noise." — **DRY** — the drop-in

Sharpened from `sweeper-19`. Four words, hard consonants, lands clean over the top of a
song intro. Dry on purpose: this is the one the board drops over a ramp, and any music in
the file would fight the record.

**Persona:** Jubilee Inspire — spoken, crisp, no warmth needed · **Target:** 0:03

**TTS script** — put **only these words** in `branded/sweepers/<name>.txt`:
```
All worship. No noise. KJubilee, H-M three-oh-eight seventy.
```

**Delivery note for the TTS voice:** crisp and even, no rise at the end, hard stop after
"noise". Close-mic, dry, no reverb — it will be sitting on top of a record.

### SW-10 · "Turn It Up in the House" — **DRY** — the energy one

`sweeper-07` was "KJubilee. Turn it up." — true but generic; every station in the world has
run it. Three extra words make it the station's own, because *the house* is already the
slogan and it works in both directions: the household of faith, and the actual room.

**Persona:** Caleb Inspire — warm rasped baritone, bright and forward · **Target:** 0:04

**TTS script** — only these words:
```
Turn it up in the house. KJubilee, H-M three-oh-eight seventy.
```

**Delivery note:** bright and forward, lift on "up", grin in it. This is the one male voice
in the sweeper set — put it in daylight hours where the contrast reads as variety rather
than as a different station.

## Rotation

| Hours | Draw from |
|---|---|
| 06:00–10:00 | SW-01, SW-02, SW-04, SW-10, SW-07 |
| 10:00–16:00 | SW-01, SW-03, SW-02, SW-08, SW-07, SW-05 |
| 16:00–20:00 | SW-02, SW-01, SW-05, SW-03, SW-10 |
| 20:00–24:00 | SW-05, SW-06, SW-02, SW-09 |
| 00:00–06:00 | SW-09, SW-06, SW-01 |

**Never twice in a row, and never the same one twice in an hour.** With ten in the pool
and five plays an hour, an unweighted random picker will collide inside a single hour more
often than feels random — weight it against the last three played.
