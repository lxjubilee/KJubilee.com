## 1. What This Is, and What It Is Not

### 1.1 The two models studied

*Verified.* Two Christian music awards were examined before settling this structure.

**The GMA Dove Awards.** The Gospel Music Association's industry award, running since 1969, voted by GMA members. Peers judging peers. The 56th ceremony was held Tuesday, 7 October 2025, at Bridgestone Arena in Nashville, broadcast on TBN. Roughly forty-plus awards, **sliced by genre**: a recorded song of the year and an album of the year within pop/contemporary, rock, rap and hip-hop, inspirational, southern gospel, bluegrass and country roots, contemporary gospel, gospel worship, and Spanish language, plus general categories and craft awards.

**The K-LOVE Fan Awards.** The network's own award, **fan-voted rather than industry-voted**. The 13th annual show was held at the Grand Ole Opry and broadcast on TBN Friday, 29 May 2026, hosted by Lauren Daigle and Sadie Robertson Huff, with voting closing 22 May and a three-day fan weekend at the Gaylord Opryland, 22 to 24 May 2026. Far fewer categories, and deliberately **not** genre-sliced. It reaches beyond music into film, television, books, sports, and digital media.

### 1.2 The lesson taken

The Doves slice by genre because they judge craft within a category. The Fan Awards refuse to slice by genre because **fans do not vote by genre, they vote by what moved them**.

KJubilee's metrics are fan behavior. Therefore the Crown Awards follow the Fan Awards shape, not the Dove shape. No genre categories.

The Fan Awards' reach beyond music is also the smarter long-term model here, because Jubilee is not only a music ministry. The structure below leaves room for a translation milestone, a testimony, or a written work to be honored without restructuring anything.

### 1.3 Year one is internal, and that is fine

Twelve personas. All music Jubilee-produced and owned outright. Year one is the catalog competing against itself, judged by Jubilee listeners, on the Jubilee platform.

**This is not a problem.** Nobody is deceived about whose music it is. A label celebrating its own artists is what labels do. Third-party personas and artists are expected later, and when they arrive the program will already have a history, a format, and a settled category list rather than being invented under pressure.

**Jubilee decision:** build it now, at low stakes, while there are only twelve contenders and mistakes about categories and pacing are cheap.

## 2. The Four Families

Twenty-four awards across four families. Each family answers a different question, which is what makes this a coherent show rather than a list.

| Family                | Count        | The question it asks                   |
| --------------------- | ------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| **Crowns**      | 8            | What was best?                         |
| **Lighthouses** | 9            | How far did it reach across languages? |
| **Shofars**     | 4            | Who came to Yeshua?                    |
| **Selahs**      | 3            | What held the silence?                 |
| **Total**       | **24** |                                        |

### 2.1 Why four families and not one list

**The single-metric collapse.** Fan voting rewards familiarity. This is the same failure mode named in section 9.3 of the station guidelines. A pure popularity contest crowns whatever got the most spins, and ballads, quiet material, and new work never place.

Four families judged by four different measures prevents that. The Crowns can be popularity-driven precisely because the Shofars and Selahs are not.

### 2.2 Why twenty-four and not more

Twelve personas are the contenders in year one. Push much past twenty-four and nearly everyone wins something, which drains the meaning out of winning. Drop much below fifteen and the broadcast is over in twenty minutes.

Twenty-four keeps real competition while filling a show. Revisit the count when third-party catalog arrives, not before.

### 2.3 Naming rationale

**Crowns.** What is handed to the faithful and then laid back down. Gives the show its posture and its natural line: laying our crowns down.

**Shofars.** The horn announces. Where the Crown looks backward at what was accomplished, the Shofar looks outward and forward.

**Selahs.** The pause in the Psalms. Honors the material that popularity never rewards.

**Lighthouses.** *Noted honestly:* this is the one image that is not Hebraic and not drawn from Scripture the way the other three are. That is deliberate. The Crowns, Shofars, and Selahs all live inside the assembly. The Lighthouse stands at the edge, looking out. The shift in register fits its job.

A lighthouse is also **fixed while the ships move toward it**, which is exactly what an international catalog does. The song stays put and the languages come to it.

*Rejected during selection, with reasons:* Doves (belongs to GMA). Trumpets (collides with Shofars). Beacons (workable, but less specific than Lighthouse). Ebenezer (reads as a personal name before it reads as a memorial stone). Tongues (Scripturally apt for Pentecost, but carries charismatic freight that would be misread). Nations (accurate and dull). Jubilees (using the ministry's own name as an internal category gets tangled).

### 2.4 Grammar

The family is **the Lighthouse Awards**, singular in the modifier. Each trophy is **a Lighthouse**. Do not pluralize into the category name.

Spoken form: *"the Lighthouse Award for International Song of the Year."*

## 4. The Lighthouses (9)

International catalog. Split six and three, deliberately.

### 4.1 Mirrored categories (6)

Judged across all non-English catalog, so there is real competition rather than a roll call.

| # | Award                                                               |
| - | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1 | **International Song of the Year**                            |
| 2 | **International Artist of the Year**                          |
| 3 | **International Worship Song of the Year**                    |
| 4 | **International Breakout Voice**                              |
| 5 | **Translation of the Year**                                   |
| 6 | **Crossing** — the song that traveled between languages best |

### 4.2 Rotating regional awards (3)

**Named by region, not by language.** The specific languages inside each region shift as the catalog does.

| # | Award                           |
| - | ------------------------------- |
| 7 | **Voice of the Americas** |
| 8 | **Voice of Europe**       |
| 9 | **Voice of Asia**         |

### 4.3 Why the six-and-three split

The two obvious approaches both fail alone.

**Slicing purely by language** is honest and gives every language community representation, but it becomes a roll call rather than a competition, and it hard-codes the language list into the awards structure. Add a fourteenth language and you have added a fifteenth award forever.

**Mirroring the Crowns only** scales cleanly, but a language with deep catalog wins every year and smaller ones never place.

The split gets both: real competition in the six, representation in the three, and the three **rotate annually based on where the year's growth actually was**. The roll call exists without calcifying.

**Rotation rule:** the three regional slots are re-designated each year before nominations open, based on catalog growth and listenership. The region names above are the year-one set, not a permanent list.

**Not decided by vote alone.** These require human review of response data, correspondence, and testimony. Voting is input, not verdict.

## 7. How the Year Feeds the Show

This is the part that matters for programming. **The awards are not an event bolted on in one month. They are the year adding itself up.**

| Family      | Fed by                                           | Station guidelines reference |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------- |
| Crowns      | The weekly chart, spin counts, vote ratios       | §9.1, §9.2, §12.6         |
| Lighthouses | International catalog metrics, translation log   | §10.2                       |
| Shofars     | The invitation break category, response tracking | §7.2 item 5                 |
| Selahs      | Anchor passage treatments, overnight programming | §6.3, §12.6                |

**Every award traces back to something the station already does daily.** Nothing here requires a new data source. It requires that existing data be retained across the year rather than rolled off.

### 7.1 Retention requirement

The metrics in section 9 of the station guidelines must be retained at **full-year granularity** for the awards to be computable. Specify retention before the first station goes live, because a metric not retained in month two cannot be recovered in month eleven.

### 7.2 The weekly chart is the foundation

*Standard practice.* The weekly countdown specified as an appointment moment in section 12.6 of the station guidelines is the engine of the Crowns. It runs all year, builds the authority, teaches listeners that their votes move something visible (section 12.9), and produces the record the Crowns are drawn from.

**Build the chart first.** The awards show is what the chart becomes once a year.

### 7.3 Nomination window

*Standard practice, and both studied awards follow it.* Nominations announced well ahead, voting closing shortly before the ceremony. The Fan Awards closed voting 22 May for a 29 May broadcast.

**Jubilee decision:** nominations announced roughly six to eight weeks out, voting closes one week before the ceremony. The gap between close and show is a programming asset, not dead time. Use it for nominee spotlights.

## 8. Timing

### 8.1 The constraint

Two feast-anchored donation drives already occupy the calendar: spring at Shavuot and fall at Sukkot, per section 8.2 of the station guidelines. **The awards must not collide with either.** A drive and an awards push in the same window asks the listener for attention and money at once, and both suffer.

### 8.2 Recommendation

**Anchor the ceremony to the Feast of Dedication (Hanukkah), in the winter.**

Reasons: it sits far from both drives, roughly balancing the calendar into three touchpoints rather than two clustered ones. Dedication and light are the right frame for honoring a year's work. And it fits the Lighthouse family without strain.

**Flagged honestly:** the Feast of Dedication is not one of the appointed times commanded in Leviticus 23. It arose in the intertestamental period. Scripture does record Yeshua at the temple during it, in John 10:22, where it is called the Feast of Dedication. Whether to anchor a Jubilee event to it is a judgment call, not a command, and it belongs to you rather than to me.

**Alternative if that anchor is unwanted:** a fixed lunar-calendar month with no feast attached, calculated annually the same way, keeping the same distance from both drives.

### 8.3 Calculation, not storage

Same rule as the drives. The date is a **calculated field** derived from the calendar source, never a stored date. Verify the calculation annually before nominations open.

## 9. Honesty Constraints

These are not optional and they override presentation in every case.

1. **A stated metric must be the actual metric.** No fabricated chart positions, no invented request counts, no rounded-up listener figures. This extends section 9.4 of the station guidelines to the awards.
2. **Say which awards are voted and which are judged.** A listener should never be led to believe a panel award was a popular vote, or the reverse.
3. **Most Played publishes its number.** The one purely mechanical award states the count out loud, which is what licenses the rest of the show to involve judgment.
4. **Consent before any Shofar names a person.** Every time.
5. **An award may go ungiven.** If no work in a category earned it that year, say so and skip it. A category given out of obligation devalues every year it was earned.

## 10. Programming Integration

Items to fold into the station clock and the delight layer:

- **Weekly chart show.** Already specified, §12.6. This is the year-round spine.
- **Nominee spotlight breaks.** During the nomination window, a break category surfacing nominated work with the reason it was nominated.
- **Voting-open sweepers.** Branded audio for the voting window only. Retire them the moment voting closes.
- **Ceremony broadcast.** The show itself airs across the network, then becomes an on-demand item.
- **Winner tagging.** Winning songs carry the award in their metadata and in the now-playing display for the following year. *This is the compounding piece.* A Crown is worth more when listeners keep encountering it.
- **The unrepeatable lane.** §12.10. The ceremony itself is a strong candidate for material that airs once.

## 11. Room to Grow

Left open deliberately, not specified:

- **Beyond music.** The Fan Awards honor film, books, sports, and digital media. Jubilee has translation milestones, written work, and testimony that could be honored here. No music award does that. Add a family when there is something real to put in it, not before.
- **Additional families.** The naming theme has room. Only add one when it answers a **question the existing four do not ask**.
- **Third-party catalog.** When outside artists arrive, revisit the twenty-four count and whether the Crowns need an internal and external split.
